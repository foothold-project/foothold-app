The maximum ray start is body+20, so high stair/wall tops are seen. To exclude app ceilings absent from training, one upward world-collision ray at the body finds the nearest overhead underside; every grid ray starts at the lower of body+20 and that underside minus 1 mm. An upward-facing exit is skipped to retain solid obstacle tops. The common ceiling height is an approximation for sloped/partial roofs: when the body is outside a roof its edge remains a high obstacle in the grid. Per-cell upward probes are intentionally avoided because they erase thin, elevated training stair plates ahead. The existing deterministic void fallback (lowest valid height minus .7, or floor datum minus .7 for all void) remains an approximation to the training virtual floor; it does not synthesize collision support. See observations.py:109–140 and sensors/virtual_floor.py:56–83.

A delay uniformly sampled from integers 0…4 at reset advances once per .005 s physics substep, not once per .02 s policy tick (actions.py:37–72; go2_env_cfg.py:515–517). Reset and STOP fill every history slot with the default pose; STOP keeps the sampled delay. The sole added test control is the optional final `createSim` argument `{ame2DelaySteps: 0…4}`; normal callers sample at reset.

Go2HV torque curve and PD were already identical: kp=25, kd=.5, armature=.01, friction=0; hip/thigh x1=13.5,x2=30,y1=20.2,y2=23.4; calf ratio=30.1/15.70 with speeds divided and torques multiplied (unitree_go2.py:20–22,58–85; go2_actuator.py:29–48; go2_curve.py:6–16). Driving means torque×velocity>0; zero speed uses braking plateau. Training gain/mass/material randomization, USD solver joint limits and PhysX contact behavior are not reproduced by matching this torque envelope.

Fixed draws are representative samples, not the original terrain seed, all twelve terrain types or the curriculum distribution. Start on the easiest stair tile; the other tiles' spawn/goal coordinates are in metadata.json for local measurements. Stage points are not targeted across A* corners; nearby targets shrink to their remaining distance.

Checks report contracts, finite physics and numerical differences only. They do not judge walking success; that judgment remains with the user. Browser/headless CPU tests do not establish phone or real-robot behavior.

ORT Web WASM is the default AME-2 engine, `ame2Backend=f32` keeps synchronous JS, and `ame2Backend=webgpu` is an explicit comparison option. The asynchronous path permits one pending inference and drops wall-clock debt while waiting. It applies the same four physics substeps and delay history only after a valid result. STOP/reset/lifecycle locking invalidates pending results before applying any action or physics. This affects scheduling, not the 49-input/action/goal/height/delay contracts; slow hosts run simulation time more slowly. Missing assets or inference failure produce a persistent visible f32 rollback notice. Node CPU and headless browser parity/trajectory records do not establish phone behavior or walking success.
